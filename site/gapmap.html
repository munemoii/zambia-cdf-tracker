The Gap Map

The audit counted them. We name them. This map puts the two records side by side for every constituency — and shows where a completion the auditors counted still has no published name.

Two records, one gap

Counted is not the same as named

Zambia keeps two records of CDF delivery. The counted record is the Auditor-General's: inspectors assessed 4,879 CDF projects for the FY2024 report (published 23 December 2025) and counted 3,060 completed — but the report gives totals per constituency, never project names. The named record is what councils themselves publish: lists, handover and commissioning reports that name individual projects. This tracker holds 1,067 named completions, each traced to its published source.

A constituency's gap is the completions the audit counted there minus the completions published by name there. A gap is a publishing gap: it proves the name was never published, not that the work was never done. This map takes no position on whether an unnamed project was delivered — only the council can close its gap, by publishing the names.

The national picture

Counted vs named

Counted record: Auditor-General's report on the Constituency Development Fund (FY2024), published 23 December 2025. Named record: council publications tracked by this site, 2021–2026. The two records are never added together — they overlap, and each stands on its own.

The map

Every constituency, one tile

A schematic tile map: one tile per constituency, grouped by province and laid out in rough geographic order. Tiles are not constituency boundaries — no official constituency boundary map is published with this data, so the picture is a grid, and the numbers on each tile carry the story. Colour shows the share of counted completions still unnamed. Tap any tile for both of its records in full.

How to read this map

The method, and the honest edges

One rule, applied everywhere. A constituency's gap is its counted completions minus its named completions, and never less than zero. In 23 constituencies, councils have published more named completions than the audit counted — the published record runs from 2021 to 2026, while the audit assessed one cycle — and those extra names are real, but they belong to their own constituencies. They are never used to cancel another constituency's gap. That is why the unnamed total on this page, added constituency by constituency, is 2,216, while a single national subtraction (3,060 − 1,067) gives 1,993. Both numbers are printed here; neither is hidden.

Different clocks. The audit's FY2024 report assessed projects from the 2023 and 2024 funding cohorts. The named record is everything councils have published from 2021 to 2026 and this tracker has verified. A constituency can therefore be fully named against the audit while its council still has newer completions to publish — and a name published for an older project does not retroactively name an audited one. The gap measures the distance between the two records as they stand today.

Chiengi is the one grey tile: the FY2024 audit counted no completed projects there at all — it found 20 in progress and 3 never started — so there is no counted completion to name yet. Its council's published record names 19 projects, all at the approved stage.

Chipili † — the Auditor-General's FY2024 rows for Chipili are internally inconsistent as printed: the report prints more completed projects (36) than its printed total assessed (32), and a negative never-started figure. This page shows Chipili exactly as printed, flags it wherever it appears, and treats its gap as printed rather than settled — the same disclosure its constituency page carries.

Mwansabombwe ‡ — as printed in the report, its completed + in progress + never started figures sum to one less than its printed total assessed. Its completed figure (16) is used exactly as printed, with the same disclosure as its constituency page.

What would close a gap? A council publishing the names — a completion list, a handover report, a commissioning notice that names the project. The moment a name is published and verified, it joins the named record and the gap shrinks. How to read this tracker →
