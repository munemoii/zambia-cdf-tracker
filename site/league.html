The Publishing League

Not delivering, or just not publishing? This league ranks every council on the record it publishes itself — the names, statuses, funding years and amounts the public can actually check.

Publishing, measured

The record a council gives its own people

Everything on this tracker exists because somebody published it. The Auditor-General counts projects but names none of them — naming is the councils' job. This league turns the lens around: it scores each council on its own publications, exactly as they stand in this record — how many projects it has named, and how completely its lists describe them.

It measures publishing, not delivery. A council low in this league may be building a great deal — but if it does not publish the names, its residents cannot verify any of it. A council high in the league has handed its people a record they can check, project by project. The Gap Map shows the same story constituency by constituency; this page names the publishers.

The national picture

Who publishes the record

Counted from this tracker's database: every named project is credited to the publisher on its source document. Projects that reached the record through national bodies — Parliament, the ministry, the government news agency, news reports — are credited to those publishers, never to a council that did not publish them. The method is set out in full below.

The standard-setters

What a complete list looks like

Councils with at least 20 named projects in the record and a completeness score of 90 or more. Their lists are the template: projects named one by one, with a status, a funding year and an amount the public can check.

The full league

Every council, ranked on its own record

Ranked by the completeness score: the average of three shares — the share of a council's named projects whose published list states a status, the share that states a funding year, and the share that states an amount. A rank never stands alone: every number behind it is in the table. Tap any heading to re-sort; a council's rank stays with it.

Publishes for

Latest publication is the date of the newest council document behind a council's rows; where a council's documents carry no date, it says Not published. The completeness score treats the three stated shares equally — the formula and every judgement call behind this table are printed in the method section below.

The silent end

A constituency can only appear on this tracker when names are published. These are the places where that has not happened yet — stated plainly, because the silence is itself part of the record.

How to read this league

The method, and the honest edges

The completeness score. For each council: (status stated % + funding year stated % + amount stated %) ÷ 3, each share counted across the projects credited to that council. The three shares count equally. The score measures how completely a council describes what it publishes — a council that publishes few projects but describes them fully can outscore a council that publishes hundreds with no amounts. Volume is its own column, and both are printed for every row.

How a project is credited to a council. By the publisher named on its source document. A list a council issued on a ministry letterhead, or posted on its official Facebook page, is the council's publication. Tender notices a council posted on the national e-procurement portal (ZPPA e-GP) are likewise the council's own publications, carried on a national platform. Two tidying rules, applied the same way everywhere: spelling variants of a council's name are merged into one row, and a mirror of a council's own social posts is credited to that council.

What is not credited to councils. Some named projects reached this record through publishers that are not councils. They are counted here so the boundary is visible — and they are never given to a council that did not publish them:

    The status share, honestly. It is measured across what reached the record. A council list that states no status at all usually cannot enter the record at all — it waits in the tracker's held pile until a status is published — so almost every council scores 100% on that share. That is a fact about the record, printed here rather than smoothed over; the differences that separate councils are funding years and amounts.

    What good publishing looks like.

    • A list that names projects one by one — never totals alone.
    • A status word for every project: approved, in progress, completed.
    • The funding year printed for every project.
    • The amount printed for every project.
    • Published as a readable document — text, or a clean scan — on the council's website or official page, where the public can find it without asking.
    • Kept current: the league shows each council's latest publication date, and a list from years ago is visible as exactly that.

    What would move a council up this league? Publish the list. The moment a council publishes names with statuses, years and amounts, those projects join the record and its row rises — the sweeps that maintain this tracker read new council lists as they appear. How to read this tracker →

    Sources
