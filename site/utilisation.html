Utilisation

Where the money slows down — the journey from budget line to finished project, and how it looks through a rural lens.

The cascade

Allocated is not the same as used

Allocation is automatic — every constituency gets the same. Using the money is the hard part: Treasury has to release it, councils have to spend it, and projects have to finish. Each step leaks.

The cascade — 2025, the last complete year

Allocated by the budget, released by the Treasury, utilised as reported by MLGRD. A national utilisation rate is published only for 2025; for the other years it simply is not — which is itself part of the picture.

The rural lens

Who finishes what they start?

Each province's rural population share beside the completed share of its tracked projects, ordered from most rural to most urban. Same K40 million per constituency — very different terrain.

Read it carefully: the completed share measures the published record — where councils publish handovers and commissionings, completion shows; where they publish only approval lists, the trail stops at approval. Rurality: ZamStats, 2022 Census Preliminary Report, Figure 3.7 (the final census report puts the national rural share at 55.3%).

The honest pattern: it is not the expected one. Luapula (85% rural) and Western (81% rural) show the highest completion shares in the record, while the urban Copperbelt sits near the bottom. What separates provinces today is less geography than the kind of record their councils publish — the rural question closes only when every province publishes delivery, not just approvals.
