Loans & Bursaries

The CDF money that goes to people, not projects — scholarships for learners, loans and grants for local enterprise.

Scholarships

Bursaries: school and skills

Two bursary streams run in every constituency: secondary-school boarding bursaries, and skills-development bursaries for learners at TEVET and other training institutions. They are the part of the CDF most families meet directly.

The bursary split

Cumulative beneficiaries since 2022. Skills bursaries — TEVET and trade training — now reach more Zambians than secondary boarding bursaries.

Two counts, both published

Government publishes bursary totals two ways, and they do not match — because they count different things. The cumulative figures above count all secondary and skills beneficiaries since 2022. MLGRD's September 2025 set counts the narrower boarding-only secondary stream:

The tracker shows both rather than silently picking one. Releases move in tranches: the first 2026 bursary tranche, in February 2026, sent K100 million out — K641,025 to every constituency.

Empowerment

Loans and grants

The empowerment component splits between grants to women and youth groups and soft loans to local groups and small businesses. Loans are meant to revolve — repaid money funds the next borrower — which makes the recovery rate the number that decides whether the component survives.

The loan book, visually

Recovery, year by year

Recovery is the share of loans due that borrowers actually repaid. The since-2021 line covers every loan issued since the fund's expansion.

Why new loans are paused

Recovery improved from 10.6% in 2023 to 17% in 2024, but with only about 35% of all loans issued since 2021 recovered, the government suspended the 2026 loan component. Grants were not suspended — but how much has gone out in empowerment grants nationally is not published as a single figure. That absence is part of the record, and this page will show the figure when a source publishes it.

The next layer

What isn't published yet

National totals are nearly all that is published. Per-constituency bursary and loan counts appear only inside individual council lists — the same publishing pattern as project lists, and the tracker will add them constituency by constituency under the same rules: plainly published figures in, anything uncertain held for review. One line this tracker will not cross: councils sometimes publish beneficiaries' names; this site tracks counts and amounts, never people's names.
