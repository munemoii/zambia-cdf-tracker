The 2027 Money Flow Predictor

The 2027 CDF rate is set in the national budget, presented on Friday 6 November 2026. Until it is published, run the numbers yourself — and watch how the money would flow.

What this tool is

A model, not a record

This page is a modelling tool. Nothing on it is a published 2027 figure. The 2027 per-constituency rate has not been published yet — it will be set when the national budget is presented on Friday 6 November 2026. The moment the official rate is captured from the budget speech and the Estimates, it replaces the defaults below.

Until then, the tool starts from the 2026 rate — K40 million per constituency — held across the 226 delimited constituencies. Change the rate and the constituency basis and every figure on the page recomputes. When real 2027 releases are published, they join the record on The Money and Utilisation pages; projections made here never become record figures.

The model

Set the rate, choose the basis

156 constituencies is the current basis; 226 is the gazetted delimitation. Try the combinations and see what each one costs.

—

For reference: 2026 is K40 million × 156 = K6.24 billion. Holding the rate at K40m across 226 seats costs K9.04bn; holding the total at K6.24bn across 226 seats cuts the rate to about K27.6m each.

The projection

If 2027 follows 2025's pattern

Allocating money is not the same as money moving. In 2025 — the last year with a complete published chain — 94% of the allocation was reported released to councils, and utilisation was reported at 86%. This panel applies that same published pattern to the model above.

The assumptions, plainly. The projection applies those two published percentages to the modelled envelope, both measured against the total allocation — the same presentation as the tracker's 2025 cascade on the Utilisation page. It assumes 2027 moves at 2025's pace: if releases or utilisation run faster or slower, the real figures will differ, and when 2027's own releases are published they will sit beside this scenario on the record pages.
